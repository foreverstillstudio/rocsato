## The Idea

This concept treats musical study as a gradual act of discovery rather than a catalog of offerings.

The visitor enters through the RCM monogram, passes through a moment of darkness, then arrives in a sequence of six disciplines:

1. Piano  
2. Strings  
3. Brass  
4. Woodwinds  
5. Voice  
6. Historical Performance  

Each discipline appears as a quiet, centered editorial composition: image first, then name, then the language and person behind the instruction. The pacing is intentionally patient. The site should feel composed, serious, and alive—not like a school brochure trying too hard to sell itself.

---

## Visual Direction

| Element | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| **Atmosphere** | Black chamber, antique gold, restrained warmth |
| **Typography** | Cinzel for formal classical presence; Albert Sans for human clarity |
| **Motion** | Scroll-led dissolves and dark handoffs, never decorative movement |
| **Imagery** | Intimate, cinematic studies of instruments and musicians |
| **Interface** | Sparse, editorial, quietly authoritative |

The gold is an accent, not confetti. The darkness creates the space that lets each discipline arrive.

## How the Experience Works

The page uses one long scroll track and a permanently fixed viewport.

### Prologue
- The RCM mark begins centered in a dark field.
- Scrolling moves the viewer through the monogram.
- The page takes a brief dark pause.
- “Find Your Instrument” appears, then yields to the disciplines.

### Discipline Sequence
Every discipline follows the same visual cadence:

1. **Image** enters.
2. **Discipline name and lead line** appear.
3. **Editorial description** follows.
4. **Faculty signature** resolves last.
5. The language fades first; the image falls into darkness second.
6. A restrained trace of the next discipline appears before it fully arrives.

This shared rhythm is deliberate. It gives every field equal weight while allowing the imagery and words to carry their own character.

## Design Notes for Future Work

- Preserve the **slow reveal cadence**. Faster is not better here.
- Keep the black field genuinely black; avoid filling empty space just because it exists.
- Do not add generic “music school” ornament, decorative notes, or stock recital imagery.
- Replace placeholder faculty details only with confirmed information.
- When real photography arrives, adjust each image crop individually while preserving the shared image frame.
- Test every significant change on a narrow phone viewport. The experience must remain composed with one thumb and a small screen.
